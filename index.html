Strength and mobility, 20 minutes a day

Yoga with some structure to it

Northline Yoga is short-form strength and mobility work for people who already walk, swim or lift and want their hips, hamstrings and shoulders to keep up. Every session runs 15 to 30 minutes, names the muscles it is working, and tells you what to do on the days you feel stiff.

Where the work goes

Three areas most active adults let slide

If you run, cycle, swim or lift, you already train in a narrow range of motion. These are the three places members tell us they notice the gap first.

Stronger legs and hips

Lunges, chair holds and low squats build strength at the end of your range, which is where most people are weakest. You hold positions rather than bouncing through them.

  • Split stances that load one leg at a time
  • Hip openers held for five slow breaths

Shoulders that move again

Desk work pulls the upper back forward and the shoulders in. These sequences ask the shoulder blades to slide and the chest to open without cranking the neck.

  • Overhead reaches with a strap for tight lats
  • Upper-back rotation done slowly, both sides

Steadier balance

Balance is a skill that fades quietly from your mid-thirties on. Single-leg work brings it back, and it takes very little time each week to maintain.

  • One-leg holds with a wall or chair nearby
  • Ankle and foot work before you stand up tall

How to start

Three steps, then repeat the week

You do not need to plan a month ahead. Pick one class, get three sessions into the week, and leave a day alone.

1

Pick your starting class

If you are new to yoga, start with Foundations. If you already stretch after training, Hips & Hamstrings is the more useful first pick.

2

Three sessions a week

Three is the number members stick with. Put them on the calendar in advance, ideally on days you are already training or walking.

3

Add a rest day

Mobility work is still work. One full day off, and a Long Stretch session instead of a strength one when you feel beaten up.
