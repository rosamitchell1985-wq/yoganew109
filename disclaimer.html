Consult a physician before starting

Talk to your physician before beginning this or any exercise programme. That applies to everyone, and it applies with particular force if any of the following is true of you:

  • You have an existing or recent injury, especially to the back, neck, hips, knees, shoulders or wrists.
  • You have been diagnosed with a joint, spinal, cardiovascular, neurological or respiratory condition.
  • You are pregnant, have recently given birth, or are recovering from surgery.
  • You have osteoporosis, low bone density, or a history of fractures.
  • You experience dizziness, fainting, chest pain or shortness of breath during exercise.
  • You take medication that affects blood pressure, heart rate, balance or alertness.
  • You have been inactive for a long period, or you are unsure whether exercise is safe for you.

If your clinician tells you to avoid a movement, avoid it. Their advice is specific to you; ours is not.

Stop if something hurts

Come out of any position that produces sharp, pinching, burning or electrical sensation, and do not try to stretch through it. Stop the session entirely if you feel chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, nausea, or any pain that radiates down an arm or a leg, and seek medical attention. Numbness or tingling is a stop signal, not a stretch.

A broad, tolerable stretching sensation in the belly of a muscle is normal. Joint pain is not.
