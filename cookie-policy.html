Cookie policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. On a later visit the browser hands it back, which lets the site or a third party recognise the browser. Similar technologies — local storage, pixels, and device identifiers — do much the same job and are covered by this policy too.

Cookies Northline Yoga sets

None. This site is static HTML and CSS with a small piece of JavaScript that opens the mobile menu and the FAQ answers. It does not set a cookie, does not use local storage, and does not run its own analytics.

Advertising cookies in more detail

Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on a user's prior visits to this website and to other websites on the internet. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads based on your visit to this and other sites.

You can turn personalised advertising off at https://www.google.com/settings/ads, or opt out of a range of vendors at once at www.aboutads.info. Opting out does not remove advertising; it makes what you see less tailored to you.

Controlling cookies in your browser

Every major browser lets you block cookies, block third-party cookies only, or clear the ones already stored. Blocking third-party cookies is the setting most people want: it stops the advertising cookies described above without affecting how this site works.

  • Chrome: Settings › Privacy and security › Third-party cookies.
  • Safari: Settings › Privacy › Prevent cross-site tracking (on by default).
  • Firefox: Settings › Privacy & Security › Enhanced Tracking Protection.
  • Edge: Settings › Cookies and site permissions › Manage and delete cookies.
  • iOS and Android: the same settings appear inside the browser app; system-level ad identifiers are reset or limited in the phone's own privacy settings.

Clearing cookies also clears any opt-out choices you have made, because those choices are themselves stored in a cookie. If you clear everything, set your preferences again at the two links above.

First-party and third-party cookies

The distinction matters more than the word “cookie” does. A first-party cookie is set by the site you are looking at and can only be read back by that site. A third-party cookie is set by a different domain whose content is embedded in the page — an ad, a video player, a font file — and can be read back by that domain wherever else it appears. That is the mechanism behind advertising that seems to follow you between websites.

Northline Yoga sets no first-party cookies at all. Everything described on this page is third-party, which means blocking third-party cookies in your browser removes it entirely without breaking a single feature of this site. There is no login here, no cart, and no preference we need to remember between visits.

How long cookies last

Session cookies are deleted when you close the browser. Persistent cookies carry an expiry date and stay until it passes or until you clear them. Advertising cookies are usually persistent, because their purpose is to recognise the same browser on a later visit; the exact lifetime is set by the vendor and can range from a few days to around two years. You do not have to wait for them to expire — clearing cookies in your browser removes them immediately.

What happens if you block everything

This site continues to work normally. Pages load, the mobile menu opens, the FAQ answers expand and the forms behave the same way. Two things change: any advertising you see becomes generic rather than tailored, and the browser may re-request the typefaces and photographs more often, which costs a small amount of extra loading time. Nothing is hidden behind a cookie wall, and we will not ask you to accept cookies in order to read a page.

Do Not Track

There is no agreed standard for how a site should respond to a Do Not Track browser signal, so this site does not respond to it. The controls described above are effective regardless.

Changes

If we add analytics or another service that sets cookies, this page will be updated before it goes live and the date at the top will change.
